PROJECT 03

Sales Pipeline & Commercial Performance

A case study transferred from my Google Sites portfolio.

B2B Sales Pipeline Analysis, Revenue Forecasting & Commercial Performance

Commercial Analytics | Sales Operations | Spreadsheet Modelling | Revenue Forecasting | KPI Reporting

Self-directed portfolio case study • Simulated data • 220 opportunities • January–June 2026

All company details and opportunities are fictional. Forecasts use assumed probabilities; recommendations and targets are proposed, not implemented improvements.

01 - The challenge

Aster Distribution Europe is a fictional European B2B distributor supplying brewing equipment, service parts and café accessories to independent retailers, hospitality groups and regional distributors.

Management needs to distinguish recorded sales from potential business, identify where deals are lost and decide which opportunities need attention. A large pipeline alone does not establish that future sales targets will be met.

This self-directed case study uses 220 simulated opportunities created between January and June 2026, assessed as at 30 June 2026. It represents no real employer, customers or implemented commercial programme.

02 - Approach

I generated a reproducible dataset using a fixed random seed and documented assumptions for lead sources, customer segments, deal values and sales cycles. I checked unique IDs, date relationships, closing information, loss reasons and the reconciliation of open, won and lost opportunities.

The workflow runs from opportunity records to defined KPIs, channel and segment comparisons, current-stage analysis, a probability-weighted forecast and prioritised management recommendations.

The dataset records current or final stages rather than stage transitions. I therefore analyse stage distributions and closed-deal outcomes, without presenting historical stage-to-stage conversion rates. Simulated patterns reflect the generation assumptions and are not evidence about real markets.

03 - Tools & methods

Google Sheets: a six-tab analytical workbook with a filtered opportunity table, frozen headers, consistent EUR/date formatting, validation lists, conditional formatting and editable charts.

Formulas: COUNTIFS and SUMIFS for status-based counts and values; averages for deal size; VLOOKUP for stage probabilities; IF logic for completed sales cycles and overdue or stale records.

Forecasting: open deal value multiplied by an explicit stage probability, grouped by expected closing period. Assumed probabilities are Lead 10%, Qualified 25%, Proposal 50% and Negotiation 75%. Won and lost deals are excluded from the future forecast.

JavaScript supports reproducible simulation and independent reconciliation checks. The workbook uses formulas and charts; no PivotTables or predictive model were used.

04 - Analysis

The dashboard reconciles 220 opportunities: 64 won, 83 lost and 73 open. Closed-won bookings total €746,250; open pipeline totals €1,212,450. The closed-deal win rate is 43.5%: 64 wins divided by 147 closed deals. Average won-deal value is €11,660 and the mean completed cycle across won and lost deals is 53.7 days.

“Won bookings” is a sales-value proxy, not verified accounting revenue, cash receipts or profit. The charts compare won bookings by source, win rates by segment, monthly bookings and open value by stage.

The weighted value of all open opportunities is €500,057.50, including overdue deals. The forward July–September forecast is €349,052.50 and excludes overdue expected closes. It covers 174.5% of a proposed €200,000 quarterly target; neither that target nor the probabilities is calibrated to actual business results. Forecast accuracy has not been validated out of sample.

05 - Key findings

Lead source: referrals have the highest closed-deal win rate at 66.7% (20/30), compared with 33.3% for outbound (20/60). Referrals also contribute the most won bookings: €239,800, compared with €219,000 from outbound. These differences warrant investigation rather than an assumption that channel alone causes better outcomes.

Customer segment: regional distributors have the highest average opportunity value at €25,901. Their mean completed sales cycle is 79.6 days, compared with 35.5 days for independent retailers. Larger opportunities therefore require a longer planning horizon in this simulation.

Lost opportunities: 35 of 83 lost deals (42.2%) were lost at Proposal, representing €529,300. This is a concentration of recorded losses, not a historical proposal-stage conversion rate.

Pipeline hygiene: 21 open deals worth €299,000 have overdue expected closes. Another measure identifies 27 open deals with more than 21 inactive days; these groups can overlap and should not be added together.

06 - Recommendations & business impact

Priority 1 — Review forecast hygiene. Check the 21 overdue opportunities, confirm ownership, next actions and realistic close dates. Proposed measure: review 100% of flagged overdue deals within one weekly management cycle.

Priority 2 — Investigate qualification and proposal losses. Review outbound opportunities and the 35 proposal-stage losses; check fit, pricing feedback and documented loss reasons before changing acquisition spend. Proposed measure: log a standard loss reason for 100% of newly lost deals during a 30-day pilot.

Priority 3 — Prioritise deals using value, stage and activity. Establish weekly reviews of stale and later-stage opportunities. Proposed measure: every reviewed open deal has an owner, dated next action and refreshed expected close date.

Priority 4 — Match planning horizons to segment cycles. Track distributor deals separately and compare forecasts with subsequent outcomes before recalibrating probabilities. A potential benefit is clearer resource and revenue planning; no uplift, savings or implemented improvement has been measured.

07 - What I learned

This project strengthened my ability to structure opportunity-level data and design commercial KPIs with clear denominators. I learned to separate closed sales, unweighted open pipeline and probability-weighted forecasts rather than treat them as interchangeable measures.

Analysing channel outcomes, deal sizes and completed cycles helped me translate spreadsheet results into specific questions for management. The snapshot dataset also reinforced why stage distributions cannot establish historical conversion rates.

Building an editable forecast and checking its calculations showed me the importance of transparent assumptions, date discipline and validation. The final recommendations connect observed simulated findings to practical actions and proposed measures, while keeping potential benefits separate from achieved results.
